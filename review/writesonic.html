What is Writesonic?

Writesonic launched in 2020 as a template-based AI copywriting tool — you picked "Facebook ad" or "product description," typed a few words, and got back a handful of variations. That isn't really what it sells anymore. Its own pricing page now calls it an "AI Search Visibility Platform," and the pitch has moved from "write faster" to "find out whether ChatGPT and Google's AI answers mention your brand, and fix it if they don't."

Writing is still part of the package. Every plan includes a monthly allowance of AI-written articles and site audits. But the plans are now built around tracked prompts: questions people ask AI assistants, which Writesonic checks daily to see whether your brand shows up in the answers. On top of that sit an Action Center that ranks what to fix and agentic workflows that carry out some of the fixes. Writesonic frames it as joining SEO and AEO in one place — "SEO gets you into Google. AEO gets you into AI answers." So the real question is no longer whether it's a good AI writer. It's whether you need AI-search tracking enough to pay for it.

What changed in 2026

Writesonic has repositioned from an AI writing and SEO tool into an AI search visibility (GEO/AEO) platform. The plan lineup was rebuilt around tracked prompts — Starter, Basic, Growth, and Enterprise — starting at $79/month billed annually. The old free plan and low-cost writing tiers no longer appear on the pricing page, and the free trial now runs 7 days with a card required. What Writesonic Is in 2026

Here's what the current platform covers, based on Writesonic's official plan and feature listings. Which plan you're on decides how much of it you actually get.

AI visibility tracking

This is the core of the product now. You give Writesonic a set of prompts — 50 on Starter, 100 on Basic, 200 on Growth — and it checks daily how AI assistants answer them and whether your brand is cited. On the self-serve plans that means three platforms: ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews. Enterprise extends tracking to all eight Writesonic lists, adding Google AI Mode, Perplexity, Claude, Grok, and Muse Spark. That split matters more than it looks. If your audience leans on Perplexity or Claude, self-serve won't see them.

Action Center and agentic workflows

Tracking tells you where you're missing. The Action Center is meant to tell you what to do about it, ranking opportunities — missing prompts, citation gaps, content fixes, technical issues — by likely visibility and citation impact. Growth gets a limited version (5 off-page and 5 on-page actions a month); Enterprise gets the full one. Agentic workflows, which Writesonic describes as AI agents that create content, earn citations, and optimize your site, come as trial runs on self-serve (10, 50, or 100 depending on plan) and in full only on Enterprise.

AI Article Writer

The writing tool that built Writesonic's name is still here, now metered by article count: 15 a month on Starter, 25 on Basic, 50 on Growth, with Growth able to add 20 more for $100/month. Brand-voice support scales the same way — 1, 5, or 10 writing styles.

Site audits and SEO

Every plan includes monthly site audits, from 10 audits of up to 100 pages on Starter to 50 audits of up to 2,500 pages on Growth. The technical-SEO side lives in the same dashboard as the AI-visibility data, which is the "one platform instead of three" argument Writesonic makes against pairing a separate SEO suite with a separate AEO tracker.

Integrations

Google Search Console and WordPress are available on every plan. GA4 and Looker Studio are listed for Enterprise only.

Needs a fresh pass

Earlier versions of this review covered Chatsonic, Botsonic, Photosonic, an "80+ templates" library, a native Ahrefs integration, and a credit-based usage model. None of these appear on Writesonic's current pricing page (Ahrefs comes up only as a competitor), so we can't confirm whether they're still included in today's plans or how they're priced. We've left them out until we can check them against Writesonic's own documentation.

The Plan Trap

Plan choice still decides which product you get, but the trap looks different now. It used to be judging Writesonic on a tier too cheap to show its best features. Today it's paying for AI-visibility tracking that doesn't cover the assistants your customers actually use.

All three self-serve plans track the same three platforms: ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews. Moving from Starter to Growth buys you more prompts, more articles, more seats, sentiment analysis, and a limited Action Center. It doesn't add Perplexity, Claude, Grok, or Google AI Mode — those are Enterprise-only. So before you start the trial, write down which AI tools your audience leans on. If it's mostly ChatGPT and Google, self-serve covers you. If Perplexity or Claude matter, you're looking at an Enterprise conversation or a different tool. And because the trial needs a card, set a reminder for day 6 if you're not sure you'll keep it.

Who Writesonic Is For

The repositioning changed who this tool is for. Knowing which group you're in saves you a wasted trial.

Best for

  • Marketing teams and agencies that need to see whether ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews mention their brand
  • SEO teams that want AI-visibility data next to site audits and content production
  • Brands ready to act on what they find, not just monitor it (Growth or Enterprise for the Action Center)
  • Larger organizations that need all 8 AI platforms, SSO/SAML, and compliance coverage (Enterprise)

Not for

  • Solo writers and bloggers who mainly want an AI writer — $79/month and up is a lot to pay for article generation alone
  • Teams whose audience relies on Perplexity or Claude but who can't justify Enterprise
  • Social-first creators — the plans center on search and AI visibility, not social publishing
  • Teams that need advanced sales and marketing workflow automation — Copy.ai is built for that

If what you really need is help polishing your own writing, a dedicated writing assistant like Grammarly is a better fit for that final-pass layer.

AIToolGrade Take

Writesonic in 2026 is a different purchase from the one most reviews describe. It's now an AI search visibility platform with writing built in, priced from $79/month billed annually. Its strongest case is a team that wants AI-visibility tracking, site audits, and content production in one place, and mainly cares about ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews. The consistent friction points are frequent pricing changes, which have already reshaped the lineup once this year, and the gap between self-serve and Enterprise platform coverage. The honest recommendation: list the AI assistants your customers use, then run the 7-day trial with that list in hand. If brand voice and team writing are the priority, Jasper is more focused. If the need is workflow automation for sales and marketing, Copy.ai's Workflows are stronger.

The Bottom Line

Writesonic is no longer the budget AI writer it started as. It has rebuilt itself as an AI search visibility platform that also writes and audits, and the pricing reflects that: self-serve now starts at $79/month billed annually, with no free plan. For marketing teams and agencies that want to know whether ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews recommend them, with tracking, audits, and content fixes in one dashboard, it's a well-rounded option. The Action Center is a practical step beyond monitoring alone.

The caveats are coverage and cost. Self-serve plans watch three AI platforms, not eight, and the fuller action tools sit on Growth and Enterprise. If you just need an AI writer, you'd be paying for a lot you won't use. Check the official pricing page, match the platform list to your audience, and use the 7-day trial to decide — with a reminder set, since it needs a card.
